How it works

  • Google SheetYour products, prices, stock and settings. Orders are saved here.
  • Apps ScriptA small program inside your sheet that connects it to the website. It runs as you.
  • GitHub PagesHosts the website for free, at an address like yourname.github.io/myshop-preorder.

You own all three. I set things up and keep the website up to date. After that you run the shop from your sheet and never need to touch GitHub again.

What you need

  1. A personal Google (Gmail) account
  2. An email address for GitHub. It can be the same Gmail.
  3. Your product photos (JPG, PNG, WebP or HEIC). If you want a watermark, add it before sending, because I don't add one.
  4. Your shop logo (a PNG with a transparent background, if you have one) and your main brand color

1 Send your shop details

Send me your shop name, logo and brand color. That's all for now. You type everything else into the sheet yourself.

2 Copy and fill in the sheet

  1. Open the copy link I send you and click Make a copy. You can rename it, e.g. “My Shop Pre-orders”.
  2. Click Share, enter inggo.espinosa@gmail.com, choose Editor, and click Send.
  3. Fill in the tabs. Grey columns fill themselves in, so never type in them.
    • INVENTORY: one row per product (SKU, category, name, description, price, stock).
    • SETTINGS: your tagline, Instagram/Messenger, closing date, alert email and theme.
    • TEMPLATES: your payment and pickup/delivery details.
  4. Name each photo after its SKU (e.g. stickers-001.jpg) and send them to me as a zip or a Google Drive share.

3 Turn on the shop's connection

This lets the website read your products and save orders. It runs as your Google account, so you do this step yourself. I'll send you the detailed steps. In short:

  1. In your sheet, open Extensions ▸ Apps Script, then Deploy ▸ New deployment ▸ Web app. Set Execute as to Me and Who has access to Anyone.
  2. Authorize it. Google warns that the app “isn't verified”. That's expected for a script that belongs only to your sheet: click Advanced ▸ Go to GS2Preorder (unsafe).
  3. Copy the Web app URL (it ends in /exec) and send it to me. It isn't a password.

4 Create your GitHub account

GitHub is where your website's files live. The free plan is all you need.

  1. Go to github.com/signup. You can click Continue with Google to sign up with your Gmail, or fill in the form below it.
  2. Enter your email and a password.
  3. Choose your username. Choose carefully, because it becomes part of your website's address:

    https://username.github.io/repository/

    • Use something short that matches your shop, like myshop or myshop-ph.
    • Use letters, numbers and single hyphens only, and don't start or end with a hyphen. Lowercase reads best in an address.
    • You can change it later, but your website's address changes with it, and old links stop working.
  4. Check that Your Country/Region is right. The email updates box is optional.
  5. Click Create account, then solve the puzzle GitHub shows.
  6. GitHub emails you a launch code. Type it in to verify your email. If you don't see it, check Spam and Promotions.
  7. If GitHub asks about your plans or team size, you can skip it. If asked to pick a plan, choose Free.
Two-factor authentication. GitHub may ask you to turn on a second login step (an authenticator app, a passkey, or a text message). Follow its prompts, and save the recovery codes it shows somewhere safe. They're the only way back in if you lose your phone.

5 Create the repository

A repository (“repo”) is a folder on GitHub that holds your website's files. It holds only the website's code, never your orders or customers. Those stay in your sheet.

  1. Signed in to GitHub, click the + at the top right, then New repository.
  2. Owner: leave it as your username.
  3. Repository name: your shop's name in lowercase with hyphens, e.g. myshop-preorder. This is the last part of your website's address. Leave Description blank.
  4. Choose visibility: Public. The free plan can only publish websites from public repositories.
  5. Leave the rest as it is: Add README off, No .gitignore, No license. Your site's files come from me, and an extra file here gets in the way.
  6. Click Create repository. You'll land on a page that says “Quick setup”. That's normal: the repo is empty until I upload the site.

6 Add me as a collaborator

This lets me upload your website and keep it updated. You stay the owner and can remove me anytime.

  1. In your new repository, click Settings, the last tab at the top of the repo page. On a narrow screen it may be under the ⋯ menu.
  2. In the left sidebar, under Access, click Collaborators. GitHub may ask you to confirm your password or two-factor code first. That's a normal safety check.
  3. Click Add people.
  4. In the Find people box, type Inggo.
  5. Pick me from the list. Before you click, check that it's really me: the username is exactly Inggo, with this picture. Other accounts with similar names can show up too. If you're unsure, message me before you add anyone, and I'll confirm.
  6. Click Add to repository.

You'll then see me listed as Pending Invite, “Awaiting Inggo's response”. That's all you need to do. It changes once I accept.

7 Turn on GitHub Pages

  1. Still in Settings, click Pages in the left sidebar.
  2. Under Build and deployment ▸ Source, choose GitHub Actions.

If you don't see that option yet, skip this step for now and do it after I tell you the code is uploaded.

Send me your GitHub username and repository name when you're done, e.g. “myshop / myshop-preorder”.

Questions owners usually ask
Do I need to pay Google or GitHub?
No. Google Sheets, Apps Script and GitHub Pages are free for a shop this size.
Can people see my orders on GitHub?
No. The repository holds only the website's code. Orders and customer details go straight into your sheet, which only you (and anyone you share it with) can open.
Why do I need to add you as a collaborator?
So I can upload the site and send you fixes and new features without asking for your password. You can remove me under Settings ▸ Collaborators.
Can I use my own domain, like myshop.com?
Yes. GitHub Pages supports custom domains. Ask me once the site is live.
